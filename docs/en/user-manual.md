# User manual

>
> See also: [Installation](installation.md) · [Configuration](configuration.md) · [FAQ](faq.md) · [Development](development.md)

RevScope is organized into five tabs on the bottom bar: **Drive**, **Map**, **Workshop**, **Trips**, and **Settings**. Each tab displays the active vehicle selector in its header.

## Vehicle selector

The selector is located in the header of each tab, below the title (in **Map**, top center, and it hides while navigating). It displays a colored dot (adapter connection status), a car or motorcycle icon depending on the active vehicle type, its name (long names wrap across two lines), and a down arrow. Tapping it opens the **"Select"** bottom sheet:

- List of saved vehicles (icon + name + license plate if set), with a ✓ checkmark on the active one — tapping one activates it immediately and closes the sheet.
- **"Add another vehicle"** button that navigates to the profile form.
- Bottom row **"Adapter: ‹status› · Manage"** to pair or switch Bluetooth adapters.

On a fresh process start (the app was just opened) with one or more saved vehicles, this same sheet opens automatically with an additional checkbox: **"Do not ask again at startup"**. Check it if you always use the same vehicle — you can change this later in **Settings → Vehicle → Ask for vehicle at startup**.

## Drive

The main screen. In the top-left corner, the Bluetooth icon changes shape and color depending on adapter status (gray = disconnected, amber = connecting, green = connected, red = error; TalkBack announces it in words); tapping it opens the adapter scanner. Below the top bar is the [vehicle selector](#vehicle-selector). On the right are battery voltage (if available), the **Track mode** button (checkered flag), and the **Settings** button.

Content, from top to bottom:

- **"Vehicle status" banner** (red, tappable): only appears when the active vehicle has an item requiring your attention today — an expired document or active Pico y placa (Colombian driving restriction). Tapping it takes you directly to the corresponding card in Workshop. If everything is in order, nothing is shown.
- **Active alert banner**: brief messages for detected anomalies or newly achieved 0-100/0-60 results ("🏁 0-100 in 6.42s"), visible for 5 seconds.
- **"Start GPS trip" button**: only offered when no adapter is connected or connecting. Starts a trip recorded solely with GPS + phone sensors (route, speed, lean angle, G-forces) — without requiring the ELM327. With an active GPS trip, the button changes to **"End GPS trip"** and gauges that depend on the ECU (RPM, gear, temperature, boost) are dimmed with the note "require adapter", while the speedometer uses GPS speed.
- **Trip pill** (with the adapter connected): **"● Recording trip"** or **"● Connected · no trip"**. Tapping it shows available actions:
  - **End trip (stay connected)**, with confirmation: saves the trip to history and leaves the adapter connected. Gauges, voltage, and alerts remain live, and you can read trouble codes (Workshop or `get_dtc` via MCP) without reconnecting. The notification changes to "no trip (diagnostics)" with a **Start trip** button.
  - **Start trip**: starts a new trip on the same connection (also appears as a large button while no trip is active).
  - **Disconnect adapter**: disconnects Bluetooth and ends the trip.
  - Connecting the adapter continues to start a trip automatically. With **Auto-start trip when moving** (Settings → Vehicle and garage → Tools, enabled by default), if connected without an active trip and you maintain 10 km/h for 5 s, a trip starts automatically and notifies you ("Trip started automatically"). If you end a trip while moving, you must come to a stop before auto-start can trigger again.
- **RPM gauge**, with the entire screen border lighting up as a *shift light*: accent color when reaching 95% of the redline configured for the active vehicle, and solid red upon crossing it.
- **Speedometer**, with a chip underneath indicating the source: **OBD** or **GPS** (with a ✓ when using GPS). Tap it to manually toggle between the speed reported by the ECU and phone GPS speed — useful if the adapter gives erratic readings or to compare with the [speedometer comparison](#workshop) in Workshop.
- **Calculated gear**, **engine temperature**, and **boost bar** (turbo), side by side. If the ECU does not report the required PID, the indicator displays **«—»** and the reason instead of fabricating a zero; for example, it never shows **0 °C** as if it were a real reading.
- **Side stand, clutch, and kill switch** (only with a [CAN profile](#can-bus-data) that has verified signals): a discreet row below the gear showing **Stand up / Stand down**, **Clutch in / Clutch released**, and **Kill RUN / Kill OFF**. With the side stand down and engine running, the pill turns red: **Stand down!**. If a signal stops arriving, its pill disappears rather than freezing at the last state. When the bus stops sending a signal or the adapter cannot read it, a one-time notice appears at the bottom ("No bus data for Gear; using estimate") and the dashboard falls back to standard calculation.
- **Trip score bar**: a driving style (with emoji and label, e.g. "🌿 Eco", "🔥 Aggressive") calculated live by the efficiency engine, alongside a 0-100 **Score**.
- Without an adapter (GPS-only mode), a card explains that RPM, temperature, gear, and boost require an OBD2 adapter, with a **Configure adapter** button.

## Map

Your live position and route on an OpenStreetMap base (offline once speed cameras for your area are downloaded):

- **Traveled route** drawn as a line, with a "You" marker at the current position.
- **Smart follow**: the map follows you automatically; panning with your finger pauses following, and the **my location** button (⌖) reactivates it.
- **Heading up** (🧭): rotates the map according to your direction of travel like a navigation system; tap again to return to north-up.
- **Night map** (🌙): dims the map for nighttime driving without glare.
- **Address search**: the top search field searches streets and places (Photon, using OpenStreetMap data, no account or API key required). Results are **biased by your location**, so searching "medellín" returns Medellín, Antioquia and not the one in the Philippines. Tapping a result sets it as a destination.
- **Route to destination**: in addition to search, press and hold any point on the map. RevScope calculates a driving route (OSRM), draws it, and displays **distance and estimated time** in a chip; the chip's ✕ removes it. With a destination set, the navigation button launches turn-by-turn navigation in Google Maps to that point.
- **Built-in turn-by-turn navigation**: the **Navigate** button on the route chip starts guidance directly inside RevScope without switching apps.
  - The upcoming **maneuver** appears at the top: a large arrow, distance, and street name.
  - At the bottom, **remaining trip details**: distance, time, and estimated time of arrival (ETA).
  - Instructions are **spoken aloud in Spanish** over the same audio channel as other alerts, so they play through your helmet intercom. Each turn is announced at 400 m, at 150 m, and right at the turn, without repeating in between.
  - If you leave the route, it announces it once. It does **not yet recalculate automatically**: you must set the destination again.
  - Requires an **active trip** (OBD or GPS) to receive position updates. If no trip is active, the app prompts you instead of remaining silent.
  - The ✕ on the banner ends navigation.
- Nearby **speed cameras**: each with a marker and a semitransparent circle showing the **configured alert radius** (default 250 m — adjustable in [Configuration → Speed cameras](configuration.md#speed-cameras)).
- **Current speed** in a badge in the lower-left corner.
- Floating **"Open in Maps"** button (navigation icon): with no destination set, opens your external navigation app at your last known position.

With no active trip, the map centers on your last known system location, waiting for you to start a trip (OBD or GPS).

## Workshop

The diagnostic and maintenance hub. The top displays adapter status with icon and text («Adapter connected · vLinker FS», «No adapter» with a **Connect** button), followed by the **diagnostic session** card, **quick tools** (Codes, Guided tests, Sensors and capture, and Health check, displayed in two columns or one column with large text), the vehicle's **previous sessions** (the 3 most recent and «View all»), and **More tools**, grouped into Status, Diagnostics, and Vehicle. Tools that require an adapter display «Requires adapter» with an icon when disconnected; other tools are always available. Upon connection, the **ECU Capabilities** card displays the OBD2 protocol in plain language, the number of PIDs reported by the ECU, expected or measured sample rate, and connection details. A short PID list on K-line does not by itself indicate a defect: some ECUs, such as Delphi MT05 units, report limited parameters and respond to one request at a time.

### Diagnostic session

A session gathers everything done with a vehicle while diagnosing a fault, timestamped for comparison and reporting.

- **Start diagnostics** opens **New session**: select symptoms (multiple can be chosen), describe what you observe in your own words, enter the odometer reading (auto-completed if reported by the ECU), and add notes such as «TPS replaced a few months ago». Select a **baseline check** for comparison: by default the vehicle's most recent health check, one of the 5 most recent checks, or none. **Open session** creates it. Only one open session is permitted per vehicle: if one already exists, the app prompts to «Close previous session and open this one»; the previous session retains its timeline.
- With an open session, every **code read and clear**, **health check**, and **quick capture** (including those triggered from a PC via MCP) is automatically recorded on its **timeline**. Each event includes timestamp, type icon, verdict with icon and text («Fault», «Attention», «OK»; informational logs do not display a verdict), summary, and **View details** (exact timestamp, whether recorded by the app or PC, and the saved CSV).
- **Guided tests**: **TPS sweep**, **idle and return**, **cold start**, **battery and charging**, and **MAP vs barometric pressure** (the last four detailed below). Opened from Workshop → Guided tests, from **Add to session → Guided test**, from a trouble code guide (e.g. P0122), or from a PC via MCP (`iniciar_prueba_guiada`, see [Configuration → MCP server](configuration.md#local-network-mcp-server)); the app and PC view the identical test. Before starting, the screen lists **preconditions** with icon and text and how each was verified («Engine off (RPM = 0)», «Motorcycle stationary (0 km/h)», «TPS available on this ECU (PID 11)»); if any fails, instructions explain what to resolve and **Start test** remains disabled until cleared. Here you also choose the **reference voltage for % to V conversion** (5.0 V typical; your selection is saved for that vehicle, and if a multimeter reference measurement exists in the session it is used and noted) along with **voice guidance**. During the test: «Step 2 of 5: Half» with progress bar, large instruction text, live TPS in % and V, a **countdown ring** (TalkBack announces step transitions and the last 3 seconds), a 10-second minigraph with the target step band shaded, **Repeat step**, voice toggle, and a single primary action per phase («Ready, holding position»). The ✕ «Cancel test» asks for confirmation if data has already been recorded. The screen remains on throughout the test. With engine off and ignition on, the phone vibrates and speaks each step (respecting the voice alert toggle): closed, half, wide open, and closed again, each held for 5 s after tapping «Ready», followed by an 8 s slow sweep. The app reads TPS at the adapter's maximum rate, converts % to volts using the 5.0 V reference (typical, or multimeter measured value), and compares against editable typical bands: results indicate the pattern («Low signal across entire sweep, smooth and steady: consistent with P0122», dropouts or spikes, high signal, out of range, or normal) and are saved to the timeline with CSV. At sample rates below 8 Hz (common on K-line motorcycles), dropout and dead-zone detection are flagged as low confidence. If the motorcycle moves, the test is cancelled; if connection is lost, it is marked as «incomplete» with captured data retained, and can be repeated. The **result** displays verdict with icon and text, interpretation, suggested next step, each position **measured against its band** (a bar from 0 to reference with the target band and source, e.g. «0.3–1.0 V, Typical (editable)»), verification checks, the **full series with all 5 steps shaded** and bands, **View P0122 guide**, and **Repeat test**. If no session was open, **Save to new session** creates one and stores the result.
  - **Idle and return**: engine running (over 400 RPM), motorcycle stationary, transmission in neutral (the ECU does not report neutral: the screen shows a prompt for manual confirmation). Records 45 s of idle without touching the throttle, followed by 3 return cycles of 8 s («Rev to about 3,000 RPM and release throttle abruptly»), reading RPM, TPS, and engine temperature. During idle, live RPM is displayed with the typical idle band shaded (motorcycles 1,200–1,700 RPM, cars 600–900 RPM, editable per model). Results provide mean, standard deviation, **drift** via regression (e.g. «Idle dropped from 2,010 to 1,454 RPM in 9 s (-62 RPM/s): unstable»), **oscillation** (over 100 RPM around regression line, with period), **stalls**, **idle TPS** against the closed band («below range: ECU may not recognize idle position»), and for each return cycle, the undershoot valley upon release (typical band ≥ 70% of idle), recovery time to ±10% of idle, or **stall detection** («Engine stalled 2 out of 3 times upon releasing throttle»). All bands are typical and cite their sources; verdicts are phrased as «consistent with...».
  - **Cold start**: ignition on and engine off, ideally after an overnight rest. Step 1, **Ignition on** (10 s): compares engine, intake air, and ambient temperature if reported; if engine and air differ by 5 °C or more (typical, editable), the test is labeled **«warm start»** (preconditions show an alert but permit proceeding). Step 2, **Cranking**: «Start engine now»; completes automatically once the engine runs for 2 s (above 600 RPM, typical) or via **Finish** (max 15 s), counting **attempts** and time to start. Step 3, **Warm-up**: idling up to 60 °C or until you tap **Finish** (max 10 min), with live temperature displayed. Results indicate stalls after start, the **fast-idle profile** (should taper down as engine warms), time to reach 60 °C, and temperature **jumps** (over 5 °C in under 1 s: «consistent with P0119 or intermittent connection», with **View P0119 guide**), with full RPM and temperature series. On air-cooled motorcycles, the «engine» sensor often measures cylinder head or oil temperature, which the result explicitly notes.
  - **Battery and charging**: ignition on and engine off. Does not read a standard PID: sends repeated `AT RV` queries (the voltage measured by the adapter on pin 16 of the connector) as fast as it responds, with normal telemetry polling paused; quick capture cannot run concurrently. The setup notes that **on many motorcycles the headlight turns on with ignition (AHO)**, lowering resting voltage. Steps: **Ignition on** (10 s), **Cranking** (8 s: «Start engine now»; measures cranking voltage drop), **Idle** (15 s), and **Higher revs** (10 s held at ~3,000–4,000 RPM after tapping «Ready»). Typical bands for 12 V lead-acid, editable per model: resting ≥ 12.2 V with headlight (≥ 12.4 V without load, in cars), cranking drop ≥ 9.6 V, charging 13.5–14.5 V, and above 15.0 V flags **overcharging** («consistent with a voltage regulator/rectifier failing to regulate»). If the adapter **reboots during cranking** (connection lost or unresponsive for over 1 s), this is not an app bug: results report it as a finding, «severe voltage drop (typically below ~8 V, depending on adapter)», with data captured up to that point. At under 5 samples per second, cranking drop is marked as low confidence. `AT RV` measures at the connector with ±0.1–0.2 V accuracy (typical): in **Settings → Adapter voltage offset → Calibrate**, enter your multimeter reading across battery terminals with ignition on, and the app saves the offset for that vehicle (does not use `AT CV`, which writes directly to adapter memory). Results display each phase, verification checks against bands, full voltage graph with charging band shaded, and links to **View P0562/P0563 guide** as applicable.
  - **MAP vs barometric pressure**: with ignition on and engine off, no manifold vacuum exists, so MAP (PID 0B) must read atmospheric pressure. Records for 10 s and compares against the first available reference: ECU barometric pressure (PID 33, ±1 kPa), **phone barometer** (±0.5 kPa, typical), or **estimated pressure from GPS altitude** using standard atmospheric models (±3 kPa due to weather and altitude; at 1,500 m ≈ 84.6 kPa). Setup alerts if PID 33 is unavailable; GPS altitude requires location permission and a fix less than 1 hour old. Difference is evaluated against ±3 kPa (typical, editable) **plus source uncertainty**: 70 kPa vs 84.6 kPa estimated is a 14.6 kPa deviation, out of spec even with ±6 kPa combined tolerance, «consistent with a decalibrated MAP sensor (P0106) or low 5 V reference» (MAP shares its reference with TPS). If deviation falls within uncertainty margins, the result is marked inconclusive. Many ECUs determine barometric pressure from the MAP sensor when ignition is turned on: if PID 33 is used and the phone has a barometer, both references are cross-checked. Results show equivalent altitude for measured MAP and the series with barometric baseline ± tolerance shaded.
- **Multimeter**: opened from Workshop → Multimeter, from **Add to session → Multimeter measurement**, or from a trouble code guide («Measure with multimeter», e.g. P0122 opening the TPS template). Select the sensor (TPS, MAP, engine or air temperature, injector, or battery) and the screen displays **probe placement instructions** («back-probe connector without disconnecting, ignition on: black probe to battery negative terminal»). If the vehicle has a saved reference model with wiring data, wire colors and origin are displayed (e.g. Benelli TNT 150i: red 5 V, black ground, green-yellow signal, «owner measurement, 25-Sep-2026»); otherwise, a **generic uncolored template** is used, as wiring varies by model. Enter multimeter readings into a wire × condition table (closed, half, wide open for TPS) using decimal keyboard with comma or period support; on large text settings the table adapts into cards per wire. Each cell displays an immediate verdict with icon and text (in range, low, high, or no reference) and expected values with sources: 5 V reference 4.8–5.2 V, ground 0–0.1 V, closed TPS 0.3–1.0 V, wide open TPS 3.8–4.8 V, injector 10–16 Ω, and battery values matching the battery test, all **typical (editable)**; half throttle has no fixed band and must simply fall between closed and wide open, while MAP and temperature signal voltages lack generic baselines («compare with service manual»). The **verdict** synthesizes findings: «Reference and ground correct; low signal throughout travel: indicates faulty sensor (damaged or non-OEM replacement) or poor pin contact at connector». If a **TPS sweep** exists in the session, it compares multimeter readings with ECU reported values at closed and wide open throttle (±0.1 V typical): «ECU receives identical values to multimeter» or «points to wiring harness or ECU-side connector fault». **Save to session** logs the data to the timeline (opening a new session if none is active); a 5 V reference measured here is subsequently used to convert TPS % to volts. In **Wire colors**, you can edit individual colors and **save as model template** (marked «Edited by you», preventing app defaults from overwriting). From a PC, the MCP tool `registrar_medicion` logs cell by cell with the identical evaluation logic.
- The session overview displays status summary (state, symptoms, notes, odometer, and current trouble codes), **comparison with baseline check** (collapsible: new codes, fuel trims, voltage, temperature, and monitors, with threshold and source, e.g. «±5 pp · Typical (editable)»; a single check serves as baseline, so variations warrant attention without conclusively confirming a failure), and up to 3 **suggested tests** with rationale («due to P0122 and "Hesitation under acceleration"`).
- **Add to session** (bottom action bar) offers: Read codes, Guided test, Quick capture, Health check, Sensor snapshot (instant capture of current live values, e.g. ignition on engine off), and Note. Data can only be added to the active vehicle's open session; if viewing another vehicle's session, a notice is displayed.
- The ⋮ menu provides **Close session** (with confirmation; session becomes read-only) and **Delete session** (confirmation stating lost events and CSV files; health checks, recorded trips, and ECU stored codes remain untouched).

### Generate report

In **Workshop**, open a diagnostic session; run tests via **Add to session → Guided test** and log measurements using **Add to session → Multimeter measurement** (or directly from **Multimeter**). Open the session's ⋮ menu and tap **Generate report** to preview the HTML report. It is a self-contained file with ten sections: **Header**, **Conclusion**, **Multimeter measurement**, **What the ECU recorded** with timeline, **Comparison with previous check**, **Interpretation**, **Is this the correct replacement part?**, **Inspection order**, **Pending confirmation**, and **Sources**. From the preview you can **Edit interpretation**, **Share**, or **Print or PDF**; the print dialog supports saving directly to PDF.

CSVs attached to session events are not included in app backups. Reports can be regenerated using the backed-up session payload even if original CSV attachments are unavailable.

**Manual report testing:** generate an HTML report from a session containing data, open it in desktop Chrome, and verify proper rendering in light and dark themes (via `prefers-color-scheme`), at 360 px width, and when printing or saving as PDF.

### Model knowledge and reference values

First navigate to **Workshop → More tools → Vehicle → Vehicle profiles**, edit the profile, and select its **Reference model**. The list filters by car or motorcycle; if your exact model is not listed, leave as **No reference model** rather than selecting mismatched data.

Next open **Workshop → More tools → Vehicle → Model knowledge**. This screen compiles ECU identification, service notes, replacement part numbers, and wiring diagrams for the chosen model. Each entry cites its visible source; tap **Copy** on part numbers to copy references, and wiring data can be customized while retaining an edited indicator. Tapping **View and edit references** opens **References**, where thresholds for tests and diagnostics can be modified:

- **Typical**: general reference value; not an official manufacturer specification.
- **Source**: value accompanied by its cited origin in the app.
- **Edited by you**: value customized for this model.

The **Reset to Typical** action clears custom adjustments and restores default reference ranges. Always consult the official model service manual when factory specifications are required.

### Status

- **Vehicle status** — status grid covering Mandatory insurance (SOAT), Roadworthiness inspection (tecnomecánica, Colombia), today's Pico y placa (Colombian driving restriction), traffic fines (SIMIT, Colombia), comprehensive insurance, scheduled maintenance, and driver's license for the active vehicle, each with icon and text status («OK», «Attention», «Expired», «Not configured»), not just color alone. Expiration dates and Pico y placa city are configured in **Vehicle profiles** (Vehicle section below); detailed restriction rules are documented in [Configuration → Pico y placa](configuration.md#pico-y-placa).
- **Health check** — one-tap scan via "Scan now": with engine running, it reads trouble codes, readiness monitors (roadworthiness inspection readiness), fuel trims, oxygen sensors, battery voltage, and coolant temperature in 10–15 seconds, returning an evaluated diagnostic report with color-coded severity per item. Engine temperature is judged by the vehicle profile's **cooling** type: with liquid cooling it is a fault above 105 °C, with air/oil cooling (most motorcycles) above 130 °C. A high temperature with the engine off is reported as «Attention» (residual heat), not as a fault. If the ECU does not report a parameter, the result explicitly states **«Not available on this ECU»** rather than treating the omission as zero or a fault. Results are preserved in history and can be shared as an image (📷, to send to a mechanic) or exported to CSV.

### Diagnostics

- **Trouble codes (DTC)** — trouble code manager: **Read codes** retrieves active, pending, and permanent codes, malfunction indicator lamp status (MIL, with icon and text: «Check engine light ON · ECU reports 1 confirmed code»), and freeze frame data («stored by P0122»). Works while recording a trip or after ending one without disconnecting: background polling pauses automatically during the read.
  - **One card per code**: displays code number, mode badge with text (Active, Pending, or Permanent; codes present in multiple modes merge into a single card), SAE J2012 title, and severity icon. Codes not present in the local database are explained using standard SAE classification; manufacturer-specific codes state «consult model manual» rather than generating unverified descriptions.
  - Collapsible **Diagnostic guide** (auto-expands if only one code is present): probable causes in priority order and numbered checklist steps labeled «General guidance, not manufacturer procedure». Checked boxes save to the active Workshop session; without an open session, progress is preserved while the screen remains open and transfers to a new session if saved. Actionable steps show buttons («Start test: TPS sweep», «Measure with multimeter», «Clear codes») when corresponding tools exist in the app.
  - **Explain with AI** is optional per code (requires a configured API key); local guides operate offline without an AI key.
  - With an open session, each read logs to the timeline with its freeze frame. Without a session, **Save this reading to a new session** creates one for the active vehicle (flagging the symptom «Check engine light on» if the MIL is lit) and stores the data.
  - **Clear codes** (red button) is disabled with explanatory text whenever the ECU reports vehicle speed above 0, requiring confirmation with three warnings: readiness monitors will reset (roadworthiness inspection stations in Colombia may reject the vehicle until complete drive cycles are performed), freeze frame data is erased from the ECU (noting whether it was already saved to a session), and codes will return if faults persist. Codes can only be cleared while stationary, enforcing the same safety rules as MCP tool `borrar_dtc` (speed 0 confirmed within 2 s). If the ECU does not report recent speed (common on motorcycles or without active polling), the app requires checking «I confirm the vehicle is stationary». Following clearing, the app rescans the ECU to display before-and-after codes: if faults remain, it alerts you; if the ECU rejects the request (`7F 04 22`, conditions not correct), it explains that many ECUs require the engine to be off and confirms codes remain stored. Clearing events are recorded on the session timeline.
- **Fuel mixture and combustion** — short-term and long-term fuel trims, up to four oxygen sensors, commanded lambda, and mass air flow (MAF), evaluated live using rule-based diagnostics (lean/rich mixture, sluggish sensor, etc.) without requiring AI or an internet connection. Unsupported ECU parameters are clearly flagged as **«Not available on this ECU»**.
- **Sensor graphs** — any PID supported by the vehicle plotted on a live 30-second scrolling graph with axes and engineering units. The **ECU Capabilities** card appears at the top to inspect supported protocols and reported PIDs. The top bar button opens **Quick capture**: select 1 to 6 PIDs (or use the shortcut «Throttle and pedal (49, 4A, 11)») and the app polls them at maximum adapter throughput, displaying measured rate in Hz, a live graph, and CSV export with millisecond timestamps. Unannounced PIDs display as **«Not available on this ECU»** and cannot be selected. Graphs display large current numeric values, allow position PIDs (throttle and pedal) to be viewed **in % or volts** using editable references (or multimeter measurements from the session), shade typical TPS reference bands (closed and wide open) with sources, support **10 or 30 s** viewing windows, include **Pause graph** (freezes rendering while capture recording continues), and plot each metric in its own panel with distinct line styles (solid, dashed, dotted) and labels at the end of each line. Useful for diagnosing fast-acting sensors such as throttle pedals: with ignition on and engine off (or at idle), sweep the pedal smoothly from 0 to 100%. While running, other dashboard gauges pause. Details and safeguards are in [Configuration → OBD sampling](configuration.md#obd-sampling).
- **Advanced scanner (Mode 22)** — sweeps addresses to discover proprietary manufacturer PIDs not documented in standard OBD2 (such as riding modes or motorcycle-specific sensor channels).
- **O2 sensor waveform** — live 60-second graph of oxygen sensor voltage featuring visual lean/rich threshold bands and a toggle switch counter measuring transitions per minute (a healthy sensor switches frequently).
- **On-board monitoring results (Mode 06)** — internal diagnostic monitoring tests executed by the manufacturer's ECU, grouped by monitor identifier (MID) with current values, operating limits, and pass/fail status — useful for pre- and post-repair verification.
- **Track CAN capture** — records raw CAN bus traffic while riding without requiring Wi-Fi or a laptop, enabling later decoding of signals that only transmit while moving (such as wheel speed sensors). Requires an active adapter connection and a CAN protocol. Enter a label (defaults to `ruta`), tap **Start capture**, and ride; the screen displays elapsed time, frames received, adapter buffer overruns, and current GPS speed. **Stop and save** concludes recording, and captures stop automatically after 15 minutes if not manually halted. Recording continues with the screen off while connected to the adapter. While capturing, **standard trips are not recorded**: the adapter listens exclusively to bus traffic without polling OBD PIDs, and active trips prevent capture initialization. Alongside bus frames, it logs one GPS sample per second with speed, accuracy, and heading, **never recording geographic coordinates**; captures proceed normally without location permissions, omitting GPS fields. Upon completion, capture IDs are displayed; inspect captured data on a PC using MCP tools `leer_captura_can` and `leer_gps_captura_can` (see [Configuration → Local network MCP server](configuration.md#local-network-mcp-server)). Force-closing the app during capture discards unfinalized data.
- **AI mechanic** — interactive chat providing real vehicle context: queries transmit alongside current vehicle parameters (profile, latest health check, active DTCs, live sensor values if connected, and recent trip summaries). Requires a configured AI API key.

### Vehicle

- **Gear analyzer** — calibrates the RPM-to-speed ratio for each transmission gear, ensuring accurate gear display on the Drive dashboard.
- **Vehicle profiles** — all configured vehicles: type (car/motorcycle), fuel type, redline and maximum gauge RPM, VIN, license plate, Pico y placa city (Colombia), document expiration dates, paired Bluetooth adapter, and **Reference model**. The reference model links profiles with service notes, replacement parts, wiring diagrams, and reference thresholds; it can be set to **No reference model**. Deleting a vehicle requires confirmation; associated trips remain preserved in trip history (filtered under «All»).
- **Maintenance** — mileage-based service trackers (oil, tires, battery, chain and sprockets on motorcycles) with editable vehicle odometer, interval progress bars, and a "Log service" button per item.
- **Mileage verification** — reads factory ECU odometer values (PID `A6`, standard OBD2 supported vehicles only) and logs historical readings to detect odometer tampering: warns if mileage rolls back or advances significantly less than GPS distance tracked by the app.
- **Speedometer comparison** — displays OBD speed and GPS speed side by side in real time, calculating cumulative session averages to assess factory speedometer error.

#### CAN bus data

Many modern motorcycles and cars with CAN buses broadcast signals not exposed via standard OBD, such as true gear position (including neutral and engine-off states), side stand position, clutch switch, or kill switch status. A **CAN profile** configures RevScope to interpret these frames. The app includes a built-in profile for the **TVS Apache RTR 160 4V**.

To configure it, open **Vehicle profiles**, select the vehicle to edit, and scroll to the **CAN bus data** card:

1. Under **CAN profile**, select your model's profile (or **None** to disable CAN listening).
2. Under **Data reading mode**, select the operating mode:
   - **Standard** — standard OBD polling only. Used by default without a profile.
   - **Combined** (recommended; auto-selected with a profile) — standard OBD polling plus brief bus listening intervals between query cycles.
   - **Bus only** — continuously listens to CAN traffic; parameters not defined in the profile will not be polled.
3. Test each signal live. All profiles import with signals marked **Unverified**, and none are used until confirmed on your vehicle. Tap **Test live** next to a signal: the screen displays values in large text while instructing actions ("Move side stand and check for changes"). If readings reflect your actions, tap **Yes, it matches** and the signal is marked **Verified ✓**; otherwise tap **No, it does not match** to leave it unverified. Below, fine print notes signal parameters (e.g. `ID 500 · byte 4 · unscaled`). Testing requires an active adapter connection, ignition switch in ON position, and a CAN-equipped vehicle; if requirements are missing, instructions explain what is needed and buttons remain disabled.

Under **Advanced: signal source routing**, configure where each channel originates: **Automatic** (bus if verified; otherwise OBD; otherwise calculated estimate), **OBD**, **Bus**, **GPS** (speed only), or **Calculated** (gear only, estimated via RPM and speed). Selecting GPS for speed is equivalent to toggling the **GPS** chip on the Drive speedometer: changing one updates the other.

Bus readings share identical naming with OBD parameters, allowing dashboard gauges, trip recorders, alert engines, and MCP tools to consume them seamlessly. If a bus signal ceases transmitting (e.g. key turned OFF then ON, or reconnection), it stops displaying and RevScope reverts to standard sources: obsolete readings are never displayed as current data. On non-CAN protocols, profiles are safely ignored without generating errors.

## Trips

Recorded driving session history featuring filters and comparisons.

- **Vehicle filters**: filter chips ("All", one per saved profile, and "No vehicle") to isolate trips for a specific car or motorcycle.
- Each trip in the list displays date, adapter (or "GPS" for adapterless trips), duration, maximum RPM, top speed, and distance.
- **A/B comparison**: tap the comparison button (intertwined arrows) on a trip to flag it as "A", then tap another trip to open a side-by-side comparison. In speed graphs, trip A renders as a solid line and trip B as a dashed line.
- **Delete trip** prompts for confirmation showing date and distance: deletes telemetry, GPS track, and recorded lap times.
- With no trips recorded, the screen displays a notice with a «Start a trip» shortcut leading to Drive.

Opening a trip presents the **full report**:

- **Statistics**: duration, distance, recorded data points, top/average speed, maximum coolant temperature, peak/average RPM, maximum acceleration, and best 0-60/0-100 times if recorded.
- **Estimated fuel cost in Colombian pesos (COP)**, when sufficient telemetry exists for calculation.
- **Eco card** displaying the 0–100 trip driving efficiency score with breakdown: hard acceleration, harsh braking, sustained high-RPM time, and cruising bonus.
- **Real route map** rendered over OpenStreetMap, color-coded by speed (blue = slow, yellow = medium, red = fast).
- **Racing line**: track path with red markers highlighting hard braking zones.
- **Friction circle**: longitudinal and lateral G-force distribution with 0.5G and 1.0G reference rings.
- **Throttle vs G scatter plot**: throttle opening percentage plotted against resulting G-force to evaluate power delivery linearity.
- **Laps** (if Track mode was active): lap times with best lap indicated by ★, peak G-forces, maximum lean angle, and peak heart rate if a smartwatch was connected.
- **Graphs with axes**: RPM, speed, and — if a watch streamed data — heart rate, each plotted in its own graph with mm:ss time axes.
- **Export by metric**: export options for Speed, RPM, Temperature, Heart rate, IMU, GPS, or "All", each as an independent CSV.
- **Reassign vehicle**: car icon in the top bar to reassign the trip to a different profile (useful if you forgot to switch active profiles before driving).
- **Summary card 📷**: generates a formatted graphic summary of the trip to share via WhatsApp or social media.
